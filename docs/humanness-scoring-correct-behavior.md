# Humanness Scoring — Biological Specification

This document defines **what the block must compute and on which sequences**, described
purely at the biological level. It is the source of truth for correctness. It deliberately
avoids implementation detail (columns, parquet, tengo) — those follow from the biology, not
the other way around.

A separate review found that the current implementation scores the wrong sequences in every
modality (duplicated sequences, dropped regions, wrong order, chains merged, TCR accepted).
The "Common mistakes" section at the end maps each of those defects back to the biological
rule it violates.

---

## 1. What humanness is, and why we measure it

**Humanness** is a measure of how closely an antibody's amino-acid sequence resembles the
sequences naturally produced by the human immune system.

The reason we care is **immunogenicity risk**. Therapeutic antibodies that look "foreign"
(e.g. mouse-derived sequences from a hybridoma) can trigger an anti-drug-antibody (ADA)
response in patients — the patient's immune system attacks the drug, reducing efficacy and
causing safety problems. The closer a sequence is to the natural human antibody repertoire,
the lower this risk. **Humanization** is the engineering process of editing a non-human
antibody to raise its humanness while preserving binding; a humanness score tells you how far
you have to go and, ideally, which residues to change.

This is distinct from chemical-liability scoring (deamidation, oxidation, glycosylation,
etc.), which measures manufacturability/stability risk. Humanness measures *immunogenicity*
risk. The two are complementary.

### How the score is produced

We compare the antibody sequence against a large database of **observed human antibody
sequences** (the Observed Antibody Space / `human-oas` repertoire). A short window
(9 amino acids) is slid along the sequence one residue at a time, and we ask, for each
window: *does this exact 9-residue peptide appear somewhere in the human repertoire?* The
score is the **fraction of windows that are found in humans**, rescaled to 0–100. Higher =
more human.

The single most important consequence of this method:

> The score is only meaningful when computed over a **complete, contiguous, naturally
> occurring antibody chain sequence**. Every shortcut — scoring a fragment, gluing fragments
> together, or joining two different chains — produces 9-mer windows that either don't exist
> in nature or that span junctions that never occur biologically, and the number stops
> meaning "humanness".

## 2. Biological background: the structure of an antibody

An antibody (immunoglobulin, Ig) is built from **heavy chains** and **light chains**. For
sequence-level analysis we work with the **variable domain** of each chain — the part that
differs from antibody to antibody and that determines both antigen binding and the immune
system's view of the molecule:

- **VH** — the variable domain of the heavy chain.
- **VL** — the variable domain of the light chain (a light chain is either **kappa (κ)** or
  **lambda (λ)** — different germline families, but both are antibody light chains).

Each variable domain is a single, contiguous stretch of amino acids organized into **seven
regions in one fixed order, from N-terminus to C-terminus**:

```
FR1 — CDR1 — FR2 — CDR2 — FR3 — CDR3 — FR4
```

- **FR1–FR4 (Framework Regions):** the conserved structural scaffold. The frameworks are the
  most conserved, germline-faithful part of the sequence and carry strong humanness signal.
  **FR4** is the short framework at the C-terminus (derived from the J gene segment) — it is a
  full part of the natural sequence and must not be dropped.
- **CDR1, CDR2, CDR3 (Complementarity-Determining Regions):** the hypervariable loops that
  contact the antigen. Note **CDR1 and CDR2 are also germline-encoded** (they sit inside the V
  gene segment alongside the frameworks) and carry genuine humanness signal too. The one region
  that is **not** germline-derived is **CDR3** — generated by random V(D)J recombination and
  junctional editing (the V–(D)–J junction with random N/P additions). CDR3 is therefore by far
  the most variable region and is naturally diverse even among perfectly human antibodies,
  which is exactly why scoring CDR3 alone is uninformative.

**The full variable domain** = FR1+CDR1+FR2+CDR2+FR3+CDR3+FR4 concatenated *in that order*.
This is the natural sequence of the chain, and it is the **only correct unit to score**.

Two facts about CDR3 explain a key failure mode:
1. CDR3 alone is the *least* representative region for humanness — it is supposed to be
   variable, so scoring it against the human repertoire gives an artificially low, noisy
   number that says nothing about whether the antibody is human.
2. In many datasets CDR3 is the "assembling feature" used to group sequences into clonotypes.
   That makes CDR3 convenient as a *key*, but it is the wrong sequence to *score*.

## 3. The core principle: one chain in, one score out

There is exactly one rule the whole block hangs on:

> **The unit of scoring is one complete variable domain of one chain (FR1 through FR4 in
> natural order). Each chain is scored independently. Nothing is duplicated, nothing is
> omitted, nothing from two different chains is ever concatenated.**

Everything below is just this rule applied to each data modality.

What this rules out, concretely:

- **No fragment gluing.** Do not assemble a sequence by stitching together region pieces
  (CDR1 + CDR2 + CDR3 + FR1 + …). If a complete chain sequence is available, score it as-is.
  If only some regions are available, see §6 (do not fabricate a sequence from partial pieces
  and pretend it's the chain).
- **No duplication.** Do not score the full chain *and also* its constituent regions — that
  double-counts and corrupts the windowing.
- **No region left behind.** The scoring unit is FR1–FR4 inclusive. FR4 in particular is
  routinely forgotten; it must be present.
- **No wrong order.** Regions must be in their natural N→C order. A window that crosses a
  region boundary (e.g. the last residues of FR1 into the first of CDR1) is a *real* human
  peptide; a window crossing a *fabricated* boundary (e.g. CDR3 spliced directly onto FR1) is
  not, and it poisons the score.
- **No chain merging.** Heavy and light are separate molecules with separate germline
  repertoires. They are scored separately and reported separately.

## 4. Rules by modality

### 4.1 Bulk antibody sequencing (one chain per clonotype)

In bulk BCR-seq each clonotype is typically a **single chain** (most often the heavy chain).

- Score the **complete variable domain of that one chain** (FR1–FR4 in order).
- Produce **one humanness score per clonotype**.

### 4.2 VHH / nanobody (single-domain antibody)

A VHH (nanobody, e.g. from camelids) is a functional antibody consisting of **only a heavy
variable domain** — there is no light chain partner.

- Treat it exactly like a single chain: score the **complete VHH variable domain** (FR1–FR4).
- One score per clonotype.

### 4.3 Single-cell paired sequencing (the chains are known together)

Single-cell data gives, for each cell/clonotype, the **paired chains** that actually occur
together in one B cell: a **heavy chain** and a **light chain**. These are two **independent
axes** in the data, and it is essential not to confuse them:

- **Chain type** — heavy vs light. (Upstream this is encoded as chain `A` vs `B`, ordered by
  diversity: for immunoglobulins the more-diverse heavy chain is `A`, the light chain is `B`.)
- **Rearrangement rank within a chain type** — **primary** vs **secondary**. A cell normally
  has one productive rearrangement per chain type (the *primary*); occasionally a second, rarer
  rearrangement of the *same* chain type is detected (the *secondary*, e.g. allelic inclusion).

So the data has up to four units per cell: **Heavy-primary, Light-primary, Heavy-secondary,
Light-secondary** — "primary/secondary" is **not** a synonym for "heavy/light".

- **Score each chain separately.** Do **not** join chains into a single sequence and do **not**
  produce a single combined score.
- Report a humanness score **per chain**, distinguishing heavy vs light — so the user can see
  which chain is the non-human one.
- Each score is computed over that chain's **complete variable domain** (FR1–FR4), exactly as
  in the bulk case.
- The **primary** rearrangement of each chain type (Heavy-primary **and** Light-primary)
  normally carries the full variable domain and is what gets scored. **Secondary**
  rearrangements are, in practice, resolved only down to CDR3 by the upstream block (see §6) and
  are therefore not scoreable → `null`. The CDR3-only / null rule keys on **rank = secondary**,
  **not** on heavy-vs-light.

### 4.4 scFv (single-chain variable fragment)

> **⚠️ Scope / contract gap — confirm before implementing.** The original reviewer described
> scFv as a real case the block mis-handles, but the current upstream producer
> (`mixcr-clonotyping`) emits **no** scFv data — no `scFv-sequence` column, no VH/VL split, no
> linker. The impl's scFv branch (`workflow/src/main.tpl.tengo`) keys on
> `pl7.app/vdj/scFv-sequence`, a column that block never produces, so it is effectively dead
> code against the current upstream. **Before building scFv handling, confirm where scFv data
> actually comes from** (a different/planned producer block, and which columns identify VH, VL,
> and the linker). Until that contract exists, scFv is **out of scope**. The biology below is
> the target behavior *once* such a producer is defined.

An scFv is an **engineered** construct in which a VH and a VL are fused into one polypeptide
by an artificial **linker**. It is physically one chain, but biologically it contains two
distinct variable domains.

- **Split at the linker** and score the **VH and the VL separately**, each over its own
  complete variable domain (FR1–FR4).
- Report **two scores** (one for the heavy-derived domain, one for the light-derived domain).
- The artificial linker is **not** part of either natural variable domain and must never be
  included in a scoring window. Likewise, never let a window straddle VH→linker→VL.

## 5. What the block must refuse: TCR input

T-cell receptors (TCRs — chains TRA/TRB/TRG/TRD) have variable domains with the same
overall architecture (FR/CDR regions), so they look superficially scoreable. **They must be
rejected.**

The reason is biological: the humanness reference is a database of **human antibodies (B-cell
receptors / immunoglobulins)**. A TCR scored against an antibody repertoire produces a number
with no meaning — it is not measuring anything real. Humanness scoring applies **only to
immunoglobulins** (antibodies / BCR: IGH, IGK, IGL chains).

The block must detect TCR input and decline to process it, rather than emitting a misleading
score.

## 6. Edge cases and how to handle them

- **Sequence too short to score.** If a chain's variable domain is shorter than the 9-residue
  window, no window exists and the score is undefined → emit a **null** score for that chain
  (do not fail the whole run).
- **Only CDR3 (or other partial regions) available.** If the dataset does not provide the
  full variable domain for a chain, the humanness score for that chain is **not computable**
  in a meaningful way. The correct behavior is to emit **null** (and ideally surface why),
  **not** to score CDR3 alone and **not** to glue whatever fragments happen to be present into
  a pseudo-sequence. A low-confidence wrong number is worse than an explicit "not available".
  - *Practical note:* in single-cell data from the upstream clonotyping block, every
    **secondary-rank** rearrangement (Heavy-secondary **and** Light-secondary alike) is exported
    with **CDR3 only** (no frameworks), so its humanness is `null` by this rule. The full
    variable domain is present for the **primary** rearrangement of each chain type
    (Heavy-primary **and** Light-primary) — i.e. the CDR3-only restriction keys on **rank =
    secondary**, not on heavy-vs-light. Separately, the full domain exists at all only when the
    upstream run was assembled over the full variable region (`VDJRegion`) rather than CDR3.
    (See the migration plan for the exact data constraints.)
- **Multiple chains of the same type in one cell.** Score each real chain on its own; never
  merge them.
- **Missing FR4 (or any framework).** A variable domain missing a framework region is
  incomplete; treat as the "partial regions" case above rather than scoring the truncated
  sequence as if it were whole.

## 7. What the block delivers

Per the original block idea, the intended outputs are:

1. **Per-chain humanness score** (0–100, higher = more human), as described above — one value
   per scored chain, with chains kept distinct (primary/secondary for single-cell; VH/VL for
   scFv; the single chain for bulk/VHH).
2. **Per-residue annotation** — which specific positions in the chain look non-human. This is
   the actionable output for humanization (it tells the engineer *which* residues to change),
   and is a key differentiator from a single summary number.
3. **Liability motif flags** beyond the existing chemical-liability tooling, where the
   humanness tool surfaces them.

The score column should rank "more human" as better (higher score = lower immunogenicity
risk).

## 8. Common mistakes (mapping to the review findings)

Each item below is a real defect found in review, paired with the biological rule it breaks.

| Defect observed | Rule violated |
|---|---|
| **Bulk/VHH:** scored `full sequence + CDR1 + CDR2 + CDR3 + FR1 + FR2 + FR3` — the full chain duplicated, FR4 dropped, regions out of order | §3: one complete chain, scored once, FR1–FR4 in natural order, no duplication, no fragment gluing |
| **Single-cell:** both chains joined into one combined score | §4.3: chains scored separately, primary vs secondary kept distinct |
| **Single-cell:** when CDR3 is the assembling feature, score computed over CDR3 only | §2 + §6: CDR3 is the most variable region and is meaningless for humanness; need the full variable domain, else null |
| **scFv:** scored `Heavy CDR1 + Light CDR1 + Heavy CDR2 + Light CDR2 + …` with only FR1 kept | §4.4: split at the linker, score VH and VL separately, each FR1–FR4 in order; never interleave chains |
| **TCR accepted** | §5: humanness applies only to antibodies; TCR must be rejected |
| **Single-cell table view broken** | downstream presentation must reflect per-chain scores (§4.3/§7), not a single merged value |

---

## 9. One-paragraph summary

The block measures how human an antibody looks, to estimate immunogenicity risk. Humanness is
only meaningful when computed over a **complete variable domain of a single chain**
(FR1–CDR1–FR2–CDR2–FR3–CDR3–FR4, in that order), because the scoring method slides a 9-residue
window along the natural sequence and any fabricated junction or missing region breaks it.
Therefore: score each chain's full variable domain exactly once; never glue fragments, never
omit FR4, never reorder regions, never merge heavy and light (or VH and VL in an scFv) into
one number; report a separate score per chain; refuse TCR input.
